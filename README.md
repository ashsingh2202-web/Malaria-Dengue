📌 Project Overview
This project presents a spatial epidemiologic analysis of malaria and dengue transmission hotspots using large-scale field survey data collected across multiple study regions in India. The objective was to identify geographic risk factors, map disease burden at the village level, and generate evidence to support targeted vector control strategies and public health intervention planning.
Over 100 field surveys were conducted to identify high-risk communities, with spatial analysis used to map disease distribution and model environmental predictors of transmission.
---
🎯 Objectives
Map village-level malaria and dengue attack rates using GIS-based spatial analysis
Identify transmission hotspots and high-burden geographic clusters
Assess the relationship between environmental factors (rainfall, temperature, stagnant water) and disease incidence
Build predictive models to identify villages at highest risk for vector-borne disease
Generate actionable findings to guide targeted vector control and community health interventions
---
📊 Methods
Data Collection
Field surveys conducted across multiple districts using standardized epidemiologic questionnaires. Village-level data collected on:
Confirmed malaria and dengue case counts
Population denominators
Environmental indicators (rainfall, temperature, stagnant water presence)
Geographic coordinates (latitude/longitude)
Analytic Pipeline
Step	Method	Tool
Data Cleaning	Variable standardization, rate calculation, risk classification	SPSS
Attack Rate Calculation	Cases per 1,000 population by village	SPSS
Risk Stratification	4-tier classification (Minimal/Low/Moderate/High)	SPSS
Spatial Mapping	Village-level hotspot mapping with risk classification layers	QGIS
Environmental Analysis	Pearson correlation, independent t-tests, ANOVA	SPSS
Regression Modeling	Simple and multiple linear regression	SPSS
---
🔍 Key Findings
Malaria hotspots clustered in low-elevation villages with high stagnant water prevalence, particularly following peak monsoon months
Dengue burden correlated significantly with urban-adjacent villages and higher temperature ranges
Rainfall was a statistically significant predictor of malaria incidence (Pearson r > 0.45, p < 0.01) but showed a weaker association with dengue
Stagnant water presence was associated with significantly higher malaria attack rates (p < 0.05, two-sample t-test)
Poisson regression models identified rainfall, stagnant water, and temperature as independent predictors of both malaria and dengue incidence after adjusting for population size
High-risk village profiles were exported to support targeted vector control deployment
---
💡 Public Health Implications
Identified specific villages requiring priority vector control deployment (indoor residual spraying, larviciding)
Findings contributed to ICMR policy reporting on seasonal disease burden and geographic risk stratification
Provided evidence base for community health worker allocation in highest-burden areas
Spatial risk maps shared with district health officers to guide preventive intervention planning
